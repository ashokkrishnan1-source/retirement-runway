How to use Retirement Runway

Runway answers three questions. Will the money last? Which account should pay the bills? How much should move into a Roth each year? This guide shows where each answer is on the page, one short lesson at a time.

Open Retirement Runway

A Show me in Runway button opens Runway at the right place. ← Back to the guide brings you back to the lesson.

Start here about ten minutes

1Look around with the sample coupleNo typing yet

Runway opens with two made-up people, Dan and Linda, so you can see a finished plan before you type anything. If you have already typed your own numbers, you will see those, and this lesson works the same way.

  1. Find the two halves of the page. Your numbers are on the left and the answers are on the right. On a phone your numbers come first and the answers are below them. Tap See results ↓ to jump down.
  2. Read the headline. The label above it says how many of 1,000 simulated markets the money survived. For the sample couple it is green and says On track. The sentence under it says how long the money lasts and how much is left at the end.
  3. Look at the four boxes under the headline: Saved today, Safe yearly spending, Lifetime tax vs. typical and First required withdrawal. Lesson 3 explains them.
  4. Tap any ? on the page. Each one opens a short note: what the number is, what it assumes and what is typical.

The page at a glance. Runway has more on it than you need on day one. This is the order things come in.

2Put in your own numbersThe few that matter most

Start with the numbers below and leave everything else as it is. You can refine it later.

  1. Under Household, choose Couple or Single. Type each person's Name and Date of birth.
  2. Enter Annual income before tax (0 if you have stopped working) and Retire at age. If you are still working, also enter 401(k) contribution % and Employer match %.
  3. Enter what you have saved, by type of account, because each type is taxed differently. For each person: 401(k) / 403(b), Traditional IRA and Roth (IRA + 401k). Under Shared accounts: Cash & savings and Brokerage (taxable).
  4. Enter Social Security / mo. Use the monthly amount at full retirement age from your statement at ssa.gov. Then set Claim Social Security at age. If you have a pension, enter Pension per year as well.
  5. Set Plan to age. Pick an age you are unlikely to outlive, so the plan errs on the safe side.
  6. Under Retirement spending, enter Annual spending. This is what you spend in a year after tax, in today's dollars. Runway works out the tax and adds it.
  7. Under State taxes, choose State you live in now.

Good to know. The sample banner disappears as soon as you change a number. Runway saves your plan in this browser as you type. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Don't know a number? Tap its ? for a typical value, use that, and come back to it later.

3Read your answerThe headline and the four boxes

The top of the answers tells you where you stand. Everything there is in today's dollars: an amount thirty years away is shown as what it would buy now.

  • On track, Borderline or At riskRunway replays your plan through 1,000 made-up market histories, some kind and some rough. On track means the money lasted in at least 85 of every 100. Borderline means 70 to 85. At risk means fewer than 70.
  • Safe yearly spendingThe most you could spend each year and still have the money last in 85 of every 100 of those markets. If it is above the spending you entered, you have room. If it is below, the plan is tight.
  • Lifetime tax vs. typicalThe tax this plan saves over your lifetime, compared with the common habit of spending from the IRA first. A minus sign means you pay less. With Typical selected, the box shows that plan's total tax instead.
  • First required withdrawalIn your seventies the tax rules start forcing money out of pre-tax accounts every year. This is a required minimum distribution, or RMD. The box shows the first one and its year. A smaller one leaves you more control over your tax later.

If the headline says the money runs short, try a lower Annual spending or a later Retire at age. Under What if…, We spend it all shows a spending level that works.

Then, the question you have

Which account should pay the bills?Withdrawal plan to show · Compare the three plans

The order in which you draw on your accounts changes your lifetime tax, even with the same savings and the same spending. Runway works out three ways of doing it.

  1. Find Withdrawal plan to show at the top of the answers.
  2. Tap each choice in turn. Typical pays the bills from the IRA first. Brokerage first spends cash and the brokerage account before the IRA. Brokerage first + conversions does the same and also moves some IRA money into a Roth each year.
  3. Watch the headline and the boxes change. Every panel below follows the plan you pick.
  4. Scroll down to Compare the three plans to see all three side by side.

Good to know. Judge the plans mainly on two rows: Lifetime taxes and After-tax value to heirs. The IRA rows always look best under Typical, only because that plan empties the IRA fastest.

How much should we move into a Roth?Roth conversion plan · Find the best plan

A Roth conversion moves money from a pre-tax IRA or 401(k) into a Roth account. You pay income tax on it that year. After that it grows and comes out tax-free, for you and for your heirs. The skill is to convert in the years when your tax rate is low.

  1. Choose Brokerage first + conversions under Withdrawal plan to show. Conversions are part of that plan only.
  2. In your numbers, under Roth conversion plan, find Once either of you retires, convert each year up to. It starts at The 0% capital-gains line, the gentlest choice. The other choices fill a tax bracket and convert more.
  3. Not sure which to pick? Press Find the best conversion and Social Security settings →. Runway tries hundreds of combinations and looks for the one that leaves the most at the end of the plan, after all taxes. If it finds a better plan, Apply puts it in.
  4. For the whole schedule, scroll to the Roth conversion plan table among the yearly details. The highlighted first row is the amount for this year.

Good to know. Large conversions from age 63 can raise your Medicare premiums two years later. From age 63, convert up to and Keep income under the Medicare surcharge deal with that. The Health view shows the cost.

What do we do this year?This year view

The plan covers decades. This view turns it into this year's jobs.

  1. Tap This year in the bar above the headline.
  2. Read the boxes: what you will spend, what comes in, what comes out of savings, how much to convert and the tax.
  3. Look at the Roth conversion table. It sets your plan's amount beside other amounts, with the tax each one costs now and what it does to your lifetime tax. Type a figure in the box above the table to test your own. The button under the table puts that amount into your plan.
  4. Monthly paycheck shows where each month's money comes from and what is left after tax.
  5. To do this year lists the jobs and their dates, such as the conversion by December 31 and your tax payments.
  6. Use the Year menu to look ahead.

Good to know. Amounts in this view are in that year's dollars, so they match your statements.

When should we claim Social Security?The claiming grid · Best Social Security ages

Claiming early gives a smaller check for more years. Waiting gives a larger check for life, and after a death the survivor keeps the larger of the two checks.

  1. In the Plan view, scroll to When should you claim Social Security? The grid shows what would be left to your heirs for a few claiming ages. Higher is better.
  2. For every age from 62 to 70, go to What if… and choose Best Social Security ages. Green marks the best choice. A blue outline marks yours.
  3. To change your plan, set Claim Social Security at age for each person.

Good to know. The grid measures money left at the end. It can't know your health. If either of you expects a shorter life, claiming earlier gains ground.

Worried that benefits will be cut? Social Security cut, also under What if…, tests it.

What will health cover cost?Health view

Two health costs depend on your income, so your withdrawals and conversions change them. Runway counts both.

  1. Tap Health.
  2. Before 65. If you will retire before Medicare starts, tell Runway how you will be covered. Under Retirement spending, set Health insurance before 65 to A fixed yearly cost or Marketplace (ACA) plan. With a marketplace plan, Runway works out your subsidy, which shrinks as your income rises.
  3. From 65. Medicare charges a higher premium when your income two years earlier was above set limits. The extra is called IRMAA. The table shows each year's income, tier and cost.
  4. If conversions are costing you a subsidy or causing a surcharge, the view offers an Apply button that keeps them under the limit.

Good to know. Runway counts only the surcharge. Medicare's ordinary premiums and your other health costs after 65 belong in Annual spending.

What will the family receive?Legacy view

A dollar left in a Roth is worth more to your heirs than a dollar left in an IRA, because they pay income tax on IRA money. This view shows what they would keep.

  1. Tap Legacy.
  2. After-tax value to heirs is the main figure: everything left at the end of the plan, less the tax your heirs would pay on the IRA.
  3. The table lists each kind of account and how heirs are taxed on it.
  4. If your heirs' tax rate is different shows how the answer moves. Set your own under Heirs' tax rate % in your numbers.
  5. The Checklist covers the paperwork, starting with naming beneficiaries on every account.

Good to know. A tool for the handoff itself (who to call, where things are) is coming to this collection. It is called Open This First.

What if things change?The calculators under What if…

In the Plan view, What if… sits under the headline and its four boxes. Each calculator tests one change on a copy of your plan. Your plan stays as it is unless you press Apply.

  • We spend it allThe most you could spend each year, at different levels of caution.
  • Find the best planThe Roth and Social Security settings that leave the most after tax.
  • Spending vs heirsWhat each extra dollar of spending costs the family.
  • Retire earlier?How early you could stop working.
  • Bad market timingA market drop early in your retirement.
  • Long-term careSome years of nursing or in-home care.
  • Spend more earlyMore in the active early years, less later.
  • Best Social Security agesEvery claiming age from 62 to 70.
  • Windfall or big expenseAn inheritance, a home sale, a wedding.
  • Social Security cutBenefits reduced from a year you choose.
  • 401(k): pre-tax or RothWhich way to save while you still work.
  • Roth conversions from 63Whether to ease off conversions so Medicare costs stay down.

Tap a name to open that calculator in Runway. A few take ten to twenty seconds, because they rerun hundreds of plans.

Where do the numbers come from?Year-by-year details · How this works

Everything under Year-by-year details shows the working, one year at a time.

  • Today's $ or Future $Today's dollars take inflation out, so every amount means what it would buy now. Future dollars are the amounts your statements and tax returns will show.
  • Your tax building draws one year's tax return as a building. Deductions are the tax-free ground floor, and income fills the floors above it, each taxed at a higher rate. Slide Year to move through the plan.
  • Account balances over time shows each account in average markets. The grey band shows where 8 in 10 of the simulated markets land.
  • Taxes paid each year compares the three plans.
  • Year by year has every year of the plan in one table. Download CSV saves it for a spreadsheet.
  • Market & growth assumptions, at the bottom of your numbers, holds the returns and inflation the plan uses. Each has a ? with typical values.
  • How this works says what the model assumes and what it leaves out. Read it once.

Good to know. The line under the title at the top of Runway says which year's tax figures are in use. They are updated each November.

Keeping it

Save it, print it, move itDownloads · encrypted copies

Your plan stays in this browser on this device. To keep a copy, print it or move it to another device, save a file.

  • Download PDF makes a report you can print or give to your tax preparer.
  • Download report saves the report as a text file. Download CSV saves the year-by-year table.
  • Open saved file reopens any of those files, on any device, and picks up where you left off.
  • For a private copy, open Save or load a scenario, encrypted and online copies at the bottom of your numbers. Choose a passphrase, then Download an encrypted file. On the private claude.ai link you can also Save a version online.

A forgotten passphrase cannot be recovered. Nobody can open an encrypted copy without it. Keep an ordinary copy as well.

Take care. Reset to example brings back Dan and Linda and erases your numbers. Save a copy first.

Once a yearA November check-up

A plan is a snapshot. Go through it again each November, when most of the year's income is known and the new tax figures are in.

  1. Update your balances, your pay and your spending.
  2. Open This year and read the to-do list.
  3. Give this year's Roth conversion amount to your tax preparer, or check it in your tax software, before December 31.
  4. Download the PDF for your records and save a fresh encrypted copy.

Good to know. Run the plan again after any big change: a move, a new retirement date, an inheritance, a death in the family.

Words explained

The terms Runway usesRoth conversion, RMD, IRMAA and the rest
Roth conversion
Moving money from a pre-tax IRA or 401(k) into a Roth. You pay income tax on it that year. Afterwards it grows and comes out tax-free.
Required withdrawal (RMD)
In your seventies the tax rules start making you take a minimum amount out of pre-tax accounts every year, and pay tax on it. RMD stands for required minimum distribution.
Tax bracket
Income is taxed in layers. Each layer has its own rate, and only the dollars inside a layer pay that rate. Filling a bracket means converting until the next dollar would be taxed at a higher rate.
The 0% capital-gains line
While your taxable income stays under this line, long-term gains and qualified dividends are taxed at 0%. Converting only up to it keeps the gains in your brokerage account tax-free.
Brokerage account
An ordinary investment account with no special tax treatment. When you sell, only the gain is taxed. The gain share is the part of the account that is growth above what you paid.
Medicare surcharge (IRMAA)
An extra Medicare premium for people whose income two years earlier was above set limits. IRMAA stands for income-related monthly adjustment amount.
Marketplace subsidy
Help with the premium of a health plan bought on healthcare.gov or a state marketplace before 65. It shrinks as income rises.
Qualified charitable distribution (QCD)
A gift made straight from an IRA to a charity, allowed once you reach the qualifying age. It is not taxed and it counts toward your required withdrawal. Runway does this for you when you enter Yearly giving to charity.
Full retirement age
The age at which Social Security pays your full benefit. Claim earlier and the check is smaller for life. Wait, up to 70, and it is larger.
Today's dollars
Amounts with inflation taken out, so a figure for a year far ahead means what it would buy now.
Simulated markets
Runway replays your plan through 1,000 made-up market histories with random good and bad years. The share in which the money lasts is its measure of safety.
